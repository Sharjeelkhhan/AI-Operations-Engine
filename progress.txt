=====================================

Date: 2026-09-13
Project status: The production foundation is complete, and the AI pipeline is now built and integrated end-to-end. The project is no longer just a backend with safety checks; it now includes real extraction, retrieval, evidence analysis, decisioning, and guardrails.


1. THE BIG PICTURE
------------------
We built an AI-assisted operations backend for support and dispute workflows. The system is designed to work as a real decision engine, not a chatbot.

The platform now includes four AI capabilities working together:
1. Understand the customer claim (LLM extraction)
2. Find the relevant policy (vector retrieval)
3. Gather and interpret evidence from the database
4. Decide what should happen next with traceable reasoning

On top of that, we added guardrails so rule-based policy enforcement overrides the model when required.

This is a complete pipeline, not isolated components.

3. REAL AI PIPELINE (NOW IMPLEMENTED)
--------------------------------------
This is the most important update in the project. The AI layer is real and wired end-to-end.

3.1 Extraction: Day 2
- File: app/services/llm_service.py
- Model: Groq LLM using openai/gpt-oss-20b
- Prompt version: extract_v1
- Purpose: take a customer message and convert it into structured claim data such as:
  - claim_type
  - claimed_amount
  - urgency
  - key_details

What this does:
- Reads free-form customer complaints and normalizes them into structured fields
- Handles real-world variations such as duplicate charge, refund request, invoice mismatch, or failed payment
- Validates the output through Pydantic before returning it
- Logs latency, model name, prompt version, and token usage

3.2 Retrieval: Day 3
- Files:
  - app/services/embedding_service.py
  - app/services/retrieval_service.py
  - app/routers/policy.py
  - app/models.py
- Model: nomic-embed-text
- Storage: PostgreSQL with pgvector
- Purpose: take a customer message, embed it, and retrieve the most relevant policy chunks using cosine similarity

What this does:
- Converts the query text into a 768-dimension embedding
- Searches policy_chunks in the database using semantic similarity rather than simple string matching
- Returns ranked policy results with similarity scores
- Supports knowledge retrieval across refund, cancellation, escalation, and payment policies

3.3 Evidence gathering: Day 4
- File: app/services/evidence_service.py
- Purpose: gather structured evidence from the database and summarize it for the decision layer

What it gathers:
- customer payment records
- customer subscription records
- successful payment IDs and totals
- patterns such as repeated payments of the same value
- compact summaries for the LLM to reason over

This is not just raw row output. It detects patterns such as a likely duplicate charge and flags them clearly.

3.4 Decision engine: Day 4
- File: app/services/decision_service.py
- Model: Groq LLM using openai/gpt-oss-20b
- Prompt version: decision_v1
- Purpose: combine claim + policy + evidence into a final decision

Allowed decisions:
- REFUND_RECOMMENDED
- REFUND_WITH_APPROVAL
- CANCELLATION_REFUND
- PAYMENT_INVESTIGATION
- SECURITY_REVIEW
- HUMAN_REVIEW
- INFO_ONLY

What this does:
- Uses the extracted claim, retrieved policy chunks, and evidence bundle together
- Produces a structured decision with confidence and reason
- Requires the reason to cite specific evidence and policy reference points
- Returns a decision object that is validated by Pydantic

3.5 Guardrails: Day 4
- File: app/services/guardrail_service.py
- Purpose: enforce company policy separate from the model itself

Rules enforced:
- Unauthorized transaction claims are routed to security review
- High-value refunds require manager approval
- Low-confidence outputs are forced to human review
- Duplicate-charge claims without evidence are blocked from immediate refund approval

Why this matters:
- The LLM suggests; the rules enforce company policy.
- The model never has final authority over operational decisions.

3.6 Endpoint orchestration: Day 4
- File: app/routers/support_cases.py
- Purpose: route the full dispute workflow from a support case to the AI decision engine

Endpoint flow:
- POST /support-cases/{case_id}/extract
  - extracts the structured claim from customer text
- POST /support-cases/{case_id}/analyze
  - extracts the claim
  - retrieves relevant policy chunks
  - gathers evidence
  - decides the response
  - applies guardrails
  - returns the final structured analysis

5. WHAT THE SYSTEM ACTUALLY DOES IN PRACTICE
----------------------------------------------
A customer complaint enters the system.

Example flow:
1. Customer says they were charged twice.
2. The extraction service converts this into a structured claim.
3. Retrieval searches the policy library for the most relevant policy text.
4. Evidence service checks the customer's payment history and patterns.
5. The decision service reasons over the claim, policy, and evidence.
6. Guardrails validate the decision before returning it.
7. The API returns a structured object with the claim, decision, confidence, reason, and citations.

This is a decision engine for customer disputes, not just a text generator.
